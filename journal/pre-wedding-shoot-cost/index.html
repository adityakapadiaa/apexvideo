Pre-wedding shoot cost: what decides the price

23 November 20265 min readBy Apex Video

With Apex Video, a pre-wedding shoot usually starts from ₹85,000. Where your shoot finally lands above that depends on a handful of decisions: whether you want photographs, a film or both, how many locations and days are involved, whether a paid set or venue is part of the plan, how far the crew travels, and what you want delivered at the end. This article explains what each of those choices does to the work, and so to the pre-wedding shoot cost, so you can decide where your budget is best spent.

What does a pre-wedding shoot cost?

That figure is where a pre-wedding shoot with us usually begins. It is a starting point rather than a package, because two shoots that look similar on paper can ask very different things of a crew. A morning at one location with two outfits is a different day from a sunrise-to-sunset shoot across three places, even if both are called a pre-wedding shoot.

We would rather quote against a clear plan than against a list of features. Once we know the look you want, the places you are drawn to and what you want to keep afterwards, we can tell you what the day involves and what it costs. The sections below are the questions we ask to get there.

Photographs, a film, or both?

This is the decision that moves the price most. Photographs and film are made by different people with different equipment, and they need different things from the day. A photographer can work quickly, freezing a moment and moving on. A film needs scenes that play out in time, steady movement, clean sound if you want voices or vows, and coverage from more than one angle so the edit has something to cut between.

Apex Video shoots pre-wedding photographs as well as pre-wedding films, and many couples choose both. When both are booked together, the two crews share the same outfits, locations and light, so the day is planned once instead of twice. What we avoid is asking one crew to do both jobs at the same time. The photographs and the film each suffer when the person holding the camera is trying to do two jobs at once.

How do locations and venue fees change the price?

Every location adds travel, setting up and time, and time is what a shoot runs on. Daylight is limited, and the best of it, the first hour after sunrise and the last before sunset, is short. Two well-chosen places usually give a better result for less money than four rushed ones.

Paid sets, studios and private venues around Mumbai charge their own fees, usually by the half day or full day, and those fees sit on top of the shoot itself. They buy you privacy, a fixed schedule and shelter from the weather, which is often worth it. Public locations cost nothing to enter, but some heritage sites, cafes and private properties ask for permission or a fee for professional filming, and drones need permission within city limits. We look at these costs with you while planning, so nothing surprises you on the day. Our guide to choosing a pre-wedding shoot location in Mumbai goes through the options in detail.

One day or two, and how far from Mumbai?

A single day in the city is the simplest shoot to plan. Lonavala, Karjat and Alibaug are close enough for a long day trip, and they bring hills, farmland and beaches that Mumbai cannot. Going further, to Igatpuri, Mahabaleshwar or beyond, usually means two days, with the crew staying overnight so that both sunrise and sunset can be used.

The cost of a second day is not only the extra hours. It includes travel for the crew and equipment, stay and meals, and the time lost on the road. In return you get unhurried light and space, which is often what makes a destination shoot look the way couples imagine it. Apex Video works across India from our base in Mumbai, so a shoot further away is entirely possible once the logistics are planned.

Outfits, styling and the length of the day

Each outfit change takes time, often more than couples expect once hair, makeup and moving to a new spot are counted. A shoot with five outfits needs a longer day and more careful planning than one with two, and a longer day means more crew hours. We usually schedule outfit changes for the middle of the day, when the light is too harsh to use well, so the best light is spent on the camera rather than in the changing room.

If you are bringing a stylist or a makeup artist, their fees are separate from ours. What matters to us is that their timings fit the plan for the light.

How many people are behind the camera?

A small crew is quick, discreet and comfortable in crowded places. A larger crew gives you more: a second camera to catch reactions during a moving shot, someone dedicated to sound, someone managing lights and reflectors as the sun drops. For a film with dialogue or a choreographed sequence, the extra hands are what make the scenes look effortless. For a candid photo shoot on a quiet street, they can be too much.

The equipment follows the same logic. We bring what the plan needs, and we would rather have a lean crew that moves well than a large one that gets in the way of the couple feeling at ease.

What you receive afterwards

Much of the work in a pre-wedding shoot happens after the cameras are packed. Photographs have to be selected and finished one by one. A film has to be edited, graded for colour, set to music and, where there are voices, cleaned and mixed. A short teaser for social media, a longer cinematic film or a larger set of edited photographs each add to the hours in the edit, and so to the cost. Delivery timelines are agreed when the shoot is planned.

It helps to decide early what you will actually use. Many couples want a short film to play at the sangeet or reception and a set of photographs for invitations and save-the-dates. Knowing that before the shoot shapes the day itself.

Is a pre-wedding shoot worth the cost?

The answer depends on what you want from it. Beyond the images, a pre-wedding shoot gives a couple time in front of the camera with the same people who will later film their wedding. By the time the wedding day arrives, the crew knows how you move, which side you prefer and when you relax, and you no longer notice the lens. That familiarity shows in the wedding film.

If your budget is limited, we would suggest spending it on fewer things done well: one or two locations, good light, a small and experienced crew and one strong film or set of photographs, rather than stretching across everything. Tell us what you have in mind, and we will plan the shoot and give you a clear quote for it.
