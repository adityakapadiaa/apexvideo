Pre-wedding shoot locations in Mumbai and nearby

23 November 20266 min readBy Apex Video

The best pre-wedding shoot locations in Mumbai are the sea-facing promenades and heritage streets of South Mumbai, the forts and seafronts of Bandra, and the greenery of Sanjay Gandhi National Park. Just outside the city, Lonavala, Karjat and Alibaug give you hills, valleys and beaches within a few hours' drive. The right choice depends on the look you want, how much travel you can fit into a day, and whether you are shooting photos, a film or both.

What are the best pre-wedding shoot locations in Mumbai?

Mumbai packs a surprising range of backdrops into one city. These are the locations couples ask for most often:

  • Marine Drive. The curve of the Queen's Necklace along the Arabian Sea is at its best at sunrise, at sunset and after dark, when the street lights come on.
  • Gateway of India, Colaba and Kala Ghoda. Colonial architecture, arched colonnades and old Bombay streets for a classic, timeless look. Early mornings are far quieter than afternoons.
  • Bandra Bandstand and Bandra Fort. Sea views, rocky shoreline and the old fort walls, with cafes and lanes nearby for a more relaxed, candid feel.
  • Sanjay Gandhi National Park. Forest trails and greenery inside the city, ideal if you want nature without leaving Mumbai.
  • Madh Island and the western beaches. Quieter beaches, fishing villages and open skies, popular for beach and sunset concepts.

Which places near Mumbai work best for a pre-wedding shoot?

If you can give the shoot a full day, the hills and coast around Mumbai open up much bigger landscapes:

  • Lonavala. Hills, valleys and mist in the monsoon. It is one of the most popular pre-wedding destinations near Mumbai, and it is where we shot Harsh and Hiral's cinematic pre-wedding film.
  • Karjat. Green countryside, farms and quieter resorts, good for a rustic or village look and comfortably reached in a day.
  • Alibaug. Beaches, palm groves and sunsets. A ferry from the Gateway of India makes the journey part of the day.
  • Igatpuri and Mahabaleshwar. Further away, but worth it for misty hills and lakes if you are planning a two-day shoot.

Should you shoot in the city or outside Mumbai?

City locations are quick to reach and easy to combine, so you can move from a heritage street in the morning to a sea-facing promenade at sunset. The trade-off is crowds: popular spots fill up quickly, especially on weekends and holidays.

Locations outside Mumbai give you more privacy, bigger landscapes and fewer onlookers, which helps if you are shy in front of the camera. They need more travel time, so plan fewer locations and leave enough daylight for each one.

What is the best time for a pre-wedding shoot?

The hour after sunrise and the hour before sunset give soft, warm light that flatters skin and outfits, and mornings mean fewer people at public spots. Night shoots work well on Marine Drive and in Bandra, where the city lights become part of the frame.

Season matters too. The monsoon turns Lonavala, Karjat and the national park lush and green but brings the risk of rain on the day. The winter months usually bring clearer skies and more comfortable weather for long outdoor shoots.

Do you need permission for a pre-wedding shoot in Mumbai?

Many public places are fine for a simple shoot, but some heritage sites, private properties, cafes and resorts ask for prior permission or charge a fee for professional photography and filming. Always check before the day.

Drones need extra care. Flying a drone within Mumbai Police limits needs prior permission, and the police regularly issue temporary bans around events and festivals. If aerial shots matter to you, plan them early or choose a location outside the city where they are allowed.

Should you plan for photos, a film, or both?

Apex Video shoots pre-wedding photos and pre-wedding films, and many couples want both from the same day. A location that is perfect for a still photograph may be too noisy or crowded for a film, so it helps to pick at least one quieter spot for scenes where you speak or where sound matters.

Planning both together also saves time: the same outfits, locations and light can serve the photographs and the film, as long as the schedule allows for both.

A quick checklist for choosing your location

Before you lock in a location, run through these questions:

  • What look do you want: city, heritage, sea, hills or countryside?
  • How much travel can you fit in, and how many locations does that leave time for?
  • What time of day will you be there, and will the light suit it?
  • Does the location need permission, and are drones allowed?
  • Are you shooting photos, a film or both, and is there a quiet spot for the film?
  • Is there somewhere nearby to change outfits?
