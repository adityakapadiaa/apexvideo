Choosing a pre-wedding shoot location in Mumbai

23 November 20265 min readBy Apex Video

A location is more than a backdrop. It decides the light you shoot in, how freely you can move, how many people are watching, and what the film sounds like. Mumbai and the country around it offer heritage streets, the sea, forest and hills within a few hours of each other. After more than four decades of filming here, this is how we think about choosing between them.

What makes a good pre-wedding shoot location?

Start with light. The best pre-wedding images in Mumbai are made in the first hour after sunrise and the last hour before sunset, when the sun is low and soft. A location that faces the right way at the right time of day matters more than a famous name.

Then think about space and privacy. Most couples relax only when they stop noticing the people around them, and that relaxation is what the camera picks up. A quiet lane at seven in the morning often gives a better result than a landmark at noon.

If you are making a film as well as photographs, sound matters too. Sea wind, traffic and crowds are invisible in a still image but obvious in a film. And every move between locations costs daylight, so two well-chosen places usually give more than four rushed ones.

South Mumbai: heritage and the sea

Colaba, Kala Ghoda and the streets around the Gateway of India give a timeless, formal look: stone facades, arches, old shopfronts and long perspectives. They work best at first light, before the streets fill, and they suit structured outfits and a classic, composed style.

Marine Drive is at its finest at the two ends of the day. At sunrise the promenade is quiet and the light comes over the city; after sunset the street lights turn the curve of the bay into part of the frame. It is one of the few places in Mumbai where a night shoot genuinely works.

Bandra and the western seafront

Bandstand, Bandra Fort and the lanes around them have a looser, more candid feel. In the evening the sun sets over the sea, which gives warm backlight for photographs and silhouettes for film. The trade-off is wind and weekend crowds, so we plan film scenes with dialogue or sound elsewhere.

Further north, Madh Island and the quieter western beaches offer open sky, fishing villages and far fewer onlookers. They take longer to reach, but they give couples room to move.

Sanjay Gandhi National Park

If you want greenery without leaving the city, the national park offers forest paths and dappled light under the trees. It is calm, it suits a natural and unposed style, and it is at its best soon after the monsoon. Professional shoots here need prior permission.

Beyond the city: Lonavala, Karjat and Alibaug

Leaving Mumbai changes the scale of the images. Lonavala gives hills, valleys and, in the monsoon, low cloud rolling across the ghats. It is where we filmed Harsh and Hiral's cinematic pre-wedding film, and it rewards couples who want landscape and movement rather than architecture.

Karjat offers farmland and countryside with very few onlookers, which makes it one of the easiest places to shoot without a crowd. Alibaug brings beaches and palm groves, and taking the ferry from the Gateway of India makes the journey part of the day. Further out, Igatpuri and Mahabaleshwar are worth it for a two-day shoot.

Locations outside the city need fewer stops and more time at each one. In exchange you get privacy and space that Mumbai rarely offers.

Planning photos and a film on the same day

Apex Video shoots pre-wedding photographs and pre-wedding films, and most couples want both. The two can share outfits, locations and light, but they need different things from a place. We plan the day so that scenes with sound or dialogue happen in the quietest location, and photographs are made where the crowd or noise would not matter.

Light sets the order. Wide landscape shots are scheduled for the low sun, close and intimate moments for open shade, and outfit changes for the hours when the light is too harsh to use.

Permissions and drones

Many public places allow a simple shoot, but heritage sites, cafes, resorts and private properties often ask for prior permission or a fee for professional photography and filming. Flying a drone within Mumbai Police limits needs prior permission, and temporary bans are common around festivals and public events. If aerial shots are important to you, it is often simpler to plan them for a location outside the city.
